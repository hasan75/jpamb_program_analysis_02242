\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We asked whether the abstract state of a fixed interval analysis selects
semantics-preserving rewrite sites that reveal inconsistencies in a static
analyzer more efficiently than random or syntax-based selection. On JPAMB, with
three abstract interpreters, it does not: rewriting uncertain sites first finds
fewer distinct inconsistencies than random selection, because most of the
few inconsistencies arise only where the interval guide is certain and the
sign domain loses the compared constant. The main limitation is the size of the evidence:
micro-benchmarks, integer conditions only, analyzers from one code base, and a
pool of \Pool{} inconsistencies.

The result suggests that a guide should model the abstraction of the analyzer
under test rather than the program's uncertainty. For a sign analyzer, the
\NumStrictCrossZero{} R2-strict sites whose constant changes to or from $0$ are
such a selection, found after the fact; whether it, or settled-first selection, does better than
chance needs a planned experiment on other analyzers and larger programs.
Further work includes a loop-reshaping rule to test how widening depends on
loop shape, and a reduced product of signs and intervals as both guide and
analyzer, combining the exclusion of $0$ that signs express with the bounds
that intervals express.
